# D-0002 — A thud at the point of contact, measured with the game's own tick

**Decision**: A bundle lands where it hits and barely bounces. `Bounce`
returns 0.08 of the speed into a surface and keeps 0.2 of the slide (from
0.35 and 0.45), and `ThrownDynamite.onHitBlock` sets the bundle at the point
of contact, its box against the face, before it reflects. The landing is
held by `LandingTest`, which plays the game's projectile tick (drag 0.99,
gravity 0.03, hit found along the move) over a flat floor at four throw
angles: at most one hop, under a fifth of a block; a tumble under a block
and a half; at rest within eight ticks of landing. A gametest throws at
full speed in the arena and checks the game agrees.

**Rationale**: Rusty: "way too bouncy". Simulating the first cut at the
item's 1.5 blocks a tick showed why in numbers: a 30-degree throw hopped two
blocks, skidded thirteen and settled 36 ticks after landing. Half of that
was restitution; the other half was the game itself. `ThrowableProjectile`
finds a hit along the tick's move and leaves the projectile where the tick
began -- up to a whole tick's travel, most of a block, short of the surface
-- so a fast bundle reflected from there rose from mid-air whatever the
restitution was. Lowering the numbers alone left a hop of nearly a block.
Setting the bundle at the contact point first made the numbers mean what
they say. 0.08 and 0.2 were chosen from a table of candidates as the one
that still tumbles a little (a bundle that stops dead reads as glued) while
staying within a block and a half at the hardest throw.

**Alternatives rejected**: *Restitution alone* -- the hop stayed near a
block at speed, for the reason above. *No bounce at all* -- a thrown thing
that stops on the spot looks wrong, and D-0001's point stands: physical
enough to bounce. *Tuning by eye in the booth* -- the booth shows one throw;
the tick model covers the angles and pins the result.
